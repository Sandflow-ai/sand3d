Sculpting and bodies

An organic shape is worked like clay. Build its masses first, then work them by places along a solid's spine, never by points. A body is flesh made of fused pieces, skinned to bones and posed.

Places on a spine

Every solid has a spine, its own axis. A body runs from -Y to +Y, sweep and wire follow their path, and loft passes through the centres of its sections. Any other solid uses the longest side of its own box. scale, bend, twist and taper move the spine with the surface.

spot

spot(ob, u, side=None)

A frame on the spine at u, measured by length from 0 at its start to 1 at its end. Without a side, the frame lies on the spine with its Z along it. With a side, it lies where the surface is met going that way from the spine, its Z pointing that way: the belly, the back, a flank.

ob
The solid to read.
u
A number from 0 to 1 along the spine.
side
A direction in the space ob is in, named as bbox names them ("-Z", "+X+Z"), or (x, y, z). A side along the spine ("+Y" on a body at u = 1) meets the tip.
Limits. Read it again after an edit that moves the surface; a frame already read does not follow.

describe

describe(ob)

A text card of the solid in millimetres: its own box, where its spine runs, and at u = 0, 0.1 … 1 how far the surface is from the spine across it. Read it against a target's measures to see where a grab, an inflate or a pinch is due, and at which u.

ob
The solid to describe.

Sculpting

The sculpting verbs change a solid in place and return it. on is a frame from spot, a point, or a list of them, in the space ob is in. Their effect is full at the centre and fades to nothing at r. On a spot on the spine, they work the whole ring there, r along the spine each way.

grab, inflate, pinch, relax

grab(ob, move, r, on)
inflate(ob, by, r=None, on=None)
pinch(ob, by, r, on)
relax(ob, r=None, on=None, passes=3)

grab moves the surface by move, an (x, y, z): a snout drawn out, a back arched. inflate moves it along its normals by by, out or, below 0, in: a belly, cheeks. pinch draws it toward the spine by by, never past it: a waist, a neck, a wrist. relax evens out bumps and seams, passes times; the seam a join leaves becomes a fillet.

ob
The solid to change.
move
An (x, y, z) in the space ob is in.
by
A length; below 0, inflate hollows and pinch swells.
r
The distance at which the change fades to nothing. Without on, inflate and relax work the whole surface; relax then uses a tenth of the solid's size unless r is given.
on
Where to work; see above.
Limits. They move existing vertices: a solid with few sections has little surface to move between them. Remake it with clay first.

crease, stamp

crease(ob, path, depth, r)
stamp(ob, profile, depth, on, soft=None)

crease presses a fold along a path over the surface, depth deep on the line and nothing at r from it: a wrinkle, the line between two muscles, a lip. Below 0 it raises a ridge. stamp lays a profile on each frame of on and raises the surface under it depth along its normals: scales, warts, nostrils, the pads of a paw. Below 0 it sinks it.

path
Frames or points joined by a smooth curve, or a path such as a spline; each point is taken to the nearest surface.
profile
A closed profile: circle, rect, poly…, laid square to each frame's Z.
soft
How far the edge of a stamp is softened; a quarter of the profile's size by default.
Limits. A stamp only reaches the surface facing its frame's way.

clay

clay(ob, *others, detail=None)

Remake ob, and others fused into it, as one closed surface of even faces about detail across, as a sculptor's remesh does. Overlapping parts become one mass with no seam inside, and the sculpting verbs find surface to move everywhere.

ob
The solid to remake; it keeps its name, placement, material and spine.
others
Solids or lists of solids to fuse in; they are used up.
detail
The edge length; by default the diagonal of their box divided by 150.
Limits. Faces and edges lose their names.

Anatomy

skeleton, muscle

skeleton(**joints)
muscle(name, a, b, belly, flat=1.0, side=None, peak=0.5, bow=0.0)

skeleton names joints, read back as bones.elbow; bones.mirrored("x") gives the other side's. A muscle is a spindle between the two places it attaches, thick belly at its widest, peak of the way along, rounded to nothing at both ends. Its spine runs from a to b.

joints
Points or frames by name.
a, b
Points, joints or frames.
flat
Below 1, flattens the section toward side: a pectoral lies flat on the chest with flat=0.4, side="-Y".
side
A direction square to the muscle: "+Y", "-X+Z" or (x, y, z).
bow
Pushes the belly that far toward side, off the line between its ends.
Limits. A muscle is placed by its two ends, never by a rotation.

Flesh

A body's flesh is the surface of fused pieces. The pieces are fields, not meshes: fuse joins them and flesh makes their surface.

stack, limb, lobe, spindle

stack(name, stations, cap=None)
limb(name, joints, sections, up="+Y")
lobe(name, centre, radii)
spindle(name, a, b, sections, up="+Z")

stack is a trunk read at a few heights. limb is one piece along a smooth curve through its joints, with no ring at a joint. lobe is an ellipsoid: a skull, a muzzle, a shoulder's cap. spindle is a straight tapered piece: a foot, a hand.

stations
Rows (z, width, depth, y, n, taper): at each z its width along X and depth along Y, centred at y, a superellipse of exponent n, taper narrowing its +Y side. Every number follows a smooth curve between rows.
cap
How far the two ends of a stack are rounded; 14 mm by default.
joints
Points, joints or frames the limb passes through.
sections
For a limb, rows (distance along it, half-width, half-depth). For a spindle, rows (share of the way from a to b, half-width, half-depth, n).
up
The direction of the half-depth, carried along without twisting.
centre, radii
A lobe's centre, and its radii along X, Y and Z, or one number for a ball.

fuse, flesh

fuse(*pieces, r, wide=None)
flesh(name, *pieces, cell=None, soften=None, box=None, crisp=None)

fuse joins pieces with a smooth union: a crease r across is rounded over, and pieces further apart than r stay apart. flesh makes one closed smooth mesh where the pieces' field crosses zero, its faces named flesh.

r
The radius of the union.
wide
(point, r2, near, far): a radius r2 within near of a joint, falling back to r at far.
cell
The grid's step; 1.5 mm by default. Detail under two or three cells is lost.
soften
How far the field is blurred before the surface is taken; 2 mm by default.
box
((x0, y0, z0), (x1, y1, z1)), the grid's extent; the pieces' own with a margin by default.
crisp
[(point, near, far), ...]: the field is left sharp within near of a point, so that fingers a few cells apart stay apart.
Limits. Several pieces given to flesh are fused with r = 3 mm; fuse them first to choose.

Bones and postures

band, chain, rig

band(bone, lo, hi, normal=None, carry=(0, 1), twist=None)
chain(pieces, first, *bands, whole=None, share=None)
rig(ob, joints, bones, *chains, linear=None, smooth=None, seam=None)

rig binds a body made by flesh to bones. Each group of pieces follows a chain: its first bone, then a band for each next one, where the skin passes from the bone before to bone between lo and hi of its joint. Nothing moves until posture.

bones
{name: (joint, joint, parent)}; the root's parent is None, parents come before their children.
lo, hi
Distances from the bone's head joint, along normal. For a trunk give normal="+Z": the bands are heights.
carry
The stretch of the chain's first piece the bone holds when a posture presses parts together, in shares of its length.
twist
(bone2, a, b): bone2 follows this bone without its twist, the skin passing from one to the other between shares a and b.
whole
{piece: bone}: a piece held entire by one bone.
share
[(bone, of, joint, amount, near, far)]: bone takes amount of of's skin round a joint.
linear
[(bones, bones)]: where the skin is shared between the two sets, as at an armpit, it is blended linearly instead of with dual quaternions.
smooth, seam
How far the weights are smoothed; 3.5 mm, and (22, 36.5) mm at a linear seam, by default.

posture, detail

posture(ob, aim=None, turn=None, root=(0, 0, 0), press=True, floor=None)
detail(ob, offsets)

posture poses a rigged body from its rest pose, never from the last posture; children follow their parents. detail adds what flesh cannot say, fitted from a scan or a sculpture: one offset per vertex of the mesh flesh made. The bones carry it.

aim
{bone: direction}: a bone pointed along a direction of the world, swung the shortest way.
turn
{bone: [(axis, degrees), ...]}: turns on its joint in its parent's rest frame, the first applied last. A knee is [("x", 80)].
root
Moves the whole body, to sit it down.
press
Where a posture pushes one part into another, both skins are pressed back to meet half-way. False leaves them through one another.
floor
A hard floor at that height.
offsets
(x, y, z) per vertex, or a .npy file of them. Calling it again replaces the layer.
Limits. The same program makes the same flesh vertex for vertex, so a layer stays valid while the pieces, cell and box do not change.
